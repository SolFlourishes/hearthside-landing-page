## 💡 Definition and Foundational Theory

The concept of mindset centers on a simple belief an individual holds about their qualities, which profoundly affects the way they live their life.

* **Pervasive Influence:** This belief guides a large part of an individual's life, permeating every part of it. Much of what is considered personality actually grows out of this mindset.
* **A Simple Choice:** Mindsets are essentially beliefs—powerful ones—but they are something that resides in the mind and can be changed. The view adopted for oneself determines whether one accomplishes the things they value.
* **Scope:** People can have elements of both the fixed and growth mindsets. An individual may hold different mindsets in different areas; for example, they might believe their artistic skills are fixed but that their intelligence can be developed. Whatever mindset is held in a particular area will guide the individual in that area.

## 🚫 The Fixed Mindset

> The **fixed mindset** operates on the belief that **basic qualities are "carved in stone"**.

### Characteristics and View of Success

* **Core Belief:** Individuals believe that qualities such as intelligence or talent are fixed, inherent traits.
* **Goal of Success:** Success is defined as **proving you are smart or talented**—the goal is validation.
* **View of Effort:** Effort is viewed negatively, associated with low ability. People with this mindset believe, "If you have to work at something, you must not be good at it". They view effort as disagreeable or as something only those lacking innate talent need to expend.
* **Reaction to Failure:** Failure is intolerable because it leads to self-labeling, transforming an action ("I failed") into an **identity** ("I am a failure"). Setbacks create feelings of **utter failure and paralysis**.
* **Learning Strategies:** This mindset limits achievement and leads to **inferior learning strategies**. Individuals are primarily interested in feedback that reflects on their ability (right or wrong), but show **no sign of interest** in information that could stretch their knowledge. They view criticism as a threat or attack.
* **Self-Perception:** This mindset leads to distortion in self-perception, resulting in an illusion of knowing the "permanent truth" about oneself.

### Consequences and Contextual Examples

* **Psychological Impact:** Fixed-mindset students experience **higher levels of depression** because they ruminate over setbacks, tormenting themselves with the idea that the problem signifies they are incompetent or unworthy.
* **Leadership/Organizational Flaw:** In business, an obsession with innate "talent" (a fixed mindset) can lead to spectacular disaster, as seen in the Enron culture. Fixed-mindset leaders often avoid problems and may focus on proving their own genius.
* **Relationships:** After rejection, fixed-mindset individuals focus on **revenge**. They may have lasting, intense feelings of bitterness because the fixed mindset gives them no recipe for healing the wound.

## 🌱 The Growth Mindset

> The **growth mindset** operates on the belief that **qualities can be cultivated and improved**.

### Characteristics and View of Success

* **Core Belief:** Individuals believe that qualities, including intelligence and skills (like management), can be improved through **practice and experience**.
* **Goal of Success:** Success is defined as **stretching oneself to learn something new**—the goal is personal development. This mindset gives people courage to embrace their goals and dreams.
* **View of Effort:** Effort is seen as a **positive, constructive force**. It is viewed as transformative, possessing the power to change both one's ability and one's self. Achieving important accomplishments requires clear focus, all-out effort, and a bottomless trunk full of strategies.
* **Reaction to Failure:** Setbacks are viewed as a cue to **try harder** and are not seen as defining their identity. This mindset allows people to bounce back after poor performance.
* **Learning Strategies:** Individuals show a **passion for learning** and stretching themselves, even when the task is difficult. They seek out accurate information about their current abilities to learn effectively.
* **Self-Perception:** True self-confidence stems from the courage to be open to new ideas, reflecting a readiness to grow.

### Consequences and Contextual Examples

* **Leadership/Organizational Strength:** Participants in an experiment given a growth mindset (believing management skills could be developed) kept revising their decisions based on feedback to cultivate these skills. Growth-minded leaders view the company as an **"engine of growth"**.
* **Relationships:** Following rejection, growth-mindset individuals focus on **understanding, forgiving, and moving on**. Their primary goal is forgiveness, allowing them to learn something useful for the future.

## 🛠️ Changing Mindsets and Applied Practice

### The Process of Change

* **Conscious Awareness:** Just learning about the growth mindset can cause a significant shift. People begin to "catch themselves" in the fixed mindset—such as passing up a learning opportunity—and then actively switch themselves into the growth mindset.
* **Internal Monologue:** Mindsets frame the running internal account that monitors and interprets events.
    * The **Fixed Mindset** generates an internal monologue focused on judging (e.g., "This means I'm a loser").
    * The **Growth Mindset** generates an internal monologue focused on constructive action (e.g., "What can I learn from this? How can I improve?").
* **Praise and Messaging:** Every word and action sends a message.
    * Praising children's intelligence or talent sends a **fixed-mindset message**, making confidence fragile.
    * Feedback should instead focus on **process praise**—emphasizing the strategies, effort, or choices used—to send a growth-mindset message.

### Overcoming Obstacles with Concrete Plans

* **Ineffectiveness of Vowing:** When trying to implement change, mere intense vowing is often useless because people fail to follow through.
* **Implementation Intentions:** What works is creating a **vivid, concrete plan** about *when*, *where*, and *how* one is going to act. These plans, known as implementation intentions, lead to higher levels of follow-through and increase the chances of success, even if the person is still "feeling bad" from a setback.
