## 💡 Core Problem and Context

[cite_start]Difficulties with interpersonal communication experienced by individuals with ASD significantly contribute to their underrepresentation in the workforce and cause problems while employed[cite: 58]. [cite_start]Most research focuses on recruitment, but this study addresses the gap concerning **onboarding and retention** [cite: 59][cite_start], where individuals with ASD often suffer most from stress[cite: 60].

[cite_start]The goal is to identify ways to modify communication processes, shifting the onus **away from requiring the individual with autism to adapt** and onto changes employers can make to their HR practices[cite: 64, 71, 98]. [cite_start]This approach helps minimize perceived stress levels for employees with ASD[cite: 63, 103].

---

## 🔬 Methodology

[cite_start]The model was developed from a qualitative study based on **15 in-depth interviews** with 21 field experts, including therapists, job trainers, and employers from multiple countries[cite: 65, 66, 67]. [cite_start]The data was analyzed using Template Analysis, with codes reflecting the four stages of the employment cycle[cite: 69].

---

## 🛠️ The Inclusive Communication Model

[cite_start]The model provides concrete recommendations across the four stages of the employment cycle[cite: 70]:

### 1. Recruitment
[cite_start]The focus should be on finding candidates who can perform high-quality work with appropriate support[cite: 72].

* **Job Advertisements:** Must be **neurodiversity-friendly**. [cite_start]Use **plain language**, avoiding jargon and unnecessary qualifications[cite: 73].
* [cite_start]**Content:** Provide clear information on requirements, duties, and a precise definition of daily tasks[cite: 74].
* [cite_start]**Layout:** Use a simple layout and basic colors, avoiding flashy elements[cite: 75].
* [cite_start]**Alternative Method:** Consider transforming the ad into a request to **solve a given problem**, focusing on the best solution instead of the best CV[cite: 76].

### 2. Selection
[cite_start]The goal is to provide a friendly atmosphere where candidates can present their skills effectively[cite: 77].

* [cite_start]**Interview Structure:** Use **less structured job interviews**[cite: 78].
* [cite_start]**Instructions:** Provide verbal instructions that are **short and to the point**[cite: 78].
* [cite_start]**Questions:** Questions must be **detailed, clearly formulated, and precise**, avoiding abstraction and ambiguity[cite: 79].
* **Timing & Testing:** Provide a clear timeframe for tasks. [cite_start]**Practical skills tests** or gamification-based solutions can be effective[cite: 80].
* [cite_start]**Expert Support:** Consider conducting recruitment with the support of a specialist trained to interview candidates with ASD[cite: 81].

### 3. Onboarding
[cite_start]This stage benefits from support and the clear communication of unspoken workplace rules[cite: 82].

* [cite_start]**Support System:** Provide support from a **buddy, mentor, or job coach** to assist with new duties and clarify doubts[cite: 83].
* [cite_start]**Documentation:** Use **onboarding checklists, manuals, and guides** to organize and structure the adaptation process[cite: 84].
* [cite_start]**Unwritten Rules:** Explicitly **explain any unwritten rules** of the workplace based on organizational culture (e.g., frequency of breaks, dress code, organizational habits)[cite: 85].
* [cite_start]**Feedback Loop:** Encourage employees to ask for help and provide feedback about their needs[cite: 86].
* [cite_start]**Pre-Onboarding Training:** The process should be preceded by **"capacity building" training programs for all employees**, including management, to promote an inclusive environment[cite: 87].

### 4. Job Retention (Day-to-Day Communication)
[cite_start]The goal is to make the communication process less stressful through structure and written format[cite: 88].

* [cite_start]**Communication Mode:** Use **non-direct, electronically mediated communication** (e.g., emails, instant messaging, chatbots) as it is less stressful and promotes balanced messages[cite: 89].
* **Instruction Clarity:** Use concise and precise messages. [cite_start]**Verbal instructions should always be followed by a written form**[cite: 90]. [cite_start]Instructions should detail the task, completion method, deadline, and contact person[cite: 90].
* [cite_start]**Visual Aids:** Use visual forms such as instructional pictures or visual job schedules[cite: 91].
* [cite_start]**Structure & Change:** Introduce structure where possible, provide task sequence, and **share information about changes in advance**[cite: 92].
* **Meetings & Feedback:** Meetings should be in small groups with regularity. [cite_start]Provide a **written agenda before** the meeting and **minutes after**[cite: 93, 94].
* **Management & Feedback:** Specify a clear chain of command (ideally contact with one person). [cite_start]Feedback should be **direct but sensitive**, avoiding unnecessary emotions[cite: 95].

## ✅ Conclusions and Broader Implications

[cite_start]The study highlights that holistic interventions targeting inclusive organizational cultures are vital[cite: 97].

* [cite_start]**Focus Shift:** The focus must be on **changes employers can make** rather than expecting the individual with autism to adapt entirely[cite: 98].
* [cite_start]**Organizational Training:** The model's success relies on being preceded by tailored **sensitivity and awareness training** for individuals with ASD, their neurotypical colleagues, and managerial staff[cite: 99].
* [cite_start]**Management Role:** Job retention relies on **neurodiversity-aware managers** who provide a supportive and caring environment[cite: 100].

> **Analogy:** This Inclusive Communication Model acts like a universal adapter for the workplace. [cite_start]Instead of forcing the employee (the specialized device) to contort and change its wiring to fit every standard organizational outlet (neurotypical norms), the model adapts the workplace "outlets" (communication methods, interviews, instructions) so that the employee can plug in effortlessly, reducing the energy drain (stress) and maximizing efficiency (performance)[cite: 104].
