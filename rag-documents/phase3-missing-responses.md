## 💡 Core Concept: Missing Responses> A **missing response** is defined as a communication disruption occurring when an interlocutor does not receive a reply to a question, request, or directive, either immediately or at all.This phenomenon is a specific type of communication disruption analyzed using **Conversation Analysis (CA)**. The study of this concept investigated video recordings from an inclusive workshop in St. Petersburg, Russia, which employed neurodivergent (ND) individuals (including those with ASD) and non-diagnosed, neurotypical (NT) instructors.It's important to note that **both neurotypical and neurodivergent participants** were observed missing responses, though for different reasons and with different coping strategies.---## 💥 Interactional and Emotional Consequences### Interactional Consequences (Conversation Analysis)In Conversation Analysis (CA), conversation is built upon **adjacency pairs** (e.g., a question is the "first pair part" and the answer is the "second pair part").When a second pair part (the response) is not produced, its non-occurrence is considered a **“noticeable absence”** or **“relevant absence”**. This violation of the fundamental sequence disrupts the coherence of the conversation.### Emotional ConsequencesFailing to receive a response can frame the participant's contribution as somehow **irrelevant** and can lead the person to feel **ignored or unappreciated** on an emotional level.---## 🧠 Identified Reasons for Missing ResponsesThe analysis demonstrated various grounds for why a response might be missed in a neurodiverse interaction:* **Multiparty Interaction/Division into Pairs:** Talk coherence is challenged by the social structure (e.g., two NT instructors and two ND employees). An individual may not pay attention to the question of a person in the *other* pair.* **Prioritization Differences:** There is a difference in **prioritizing the two courses of actions**—the immediate work task versus chatting about an abstract topic—and how interlocutors switch between them.* **Unsupported Interactional Patterns:** Neurodivergent people may be involved in interactional patterns and **language games which are not supported by their neurotypical colleagues**.* **Stereotypes:** Communication can be influenced by **stereotypes** about the condition. For instance, repetitive actions (including questions) might be interpreted by a neurotypical person as **irrelevant stimming** which does not require a response.---## 🛠️ Coping Strategies (Neurotype-Specific)### Neurotypical (NT) StrategiesWhen faced with a missing response, NT participants demonstrated **more elaborate strategies**:* They **modified the question or directive**, often **including the grammatical structure**.* They utilized various ways of **drawing attention**, along with **bodily actions and argumentation**.### Neurodivergent (ND) StrategiesND participants typically tended to:* **Repeat the wording** verbatim or almost verbatim.* Generally **not modify the grammatical structure** of the main clause, or they waited.* One employee with ASD used strategies like **repeating the phrase**, adding a **vocative** (a name), adding a **politeness marker** (such as *please*), and **redirecting the request** to a specific person.---## ✅ Implications for InclusivityAwareness of these interactional challenges and how they are overcome can help secure and implement inclusivity in the workplace. One suggested solution to challenges caused by interlocutors dividing according to neurotypes and job positions is **delegating the instructor’s role to a neurodivergent person**.
