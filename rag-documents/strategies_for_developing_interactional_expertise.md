## 💡 Core Concept: Interactional Expertise

> [cite_start]**Interactional Expertise** is the capacity of communication partners to support others effectively in contributing their knowledge and ideas, particularly across different communication styles or neurotypes. [cite: 1283]

[cite_start]This document outlines strategies for developing this expertise, drawing from research on autistic/non-autistic conversations and inclusive workplace practices. [cite: 1284, 1285]

---

## 🤝 Strategies in Mixed Neurotype Conversations

[cite_start]Developing interactional expertise means actively employing "grease" (helpful) techniques and avoiding "blocks" (unhelpful) techniques. [cite: 1286]

### A. Helpful Strategies ("Grease")

[cite_start]These actions, typically by the non-autistic partner, support thinking and encourage contribution. [cite: 1287]

| Strategy Area | Specific Actions/Behaviors |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Optimizing Engagement** | [cite_start]Use cues over a series of conversational turns to enable the person to become engaged with the topic. [cite: 1289] |
| **Supporting Thinking** | [cite_start]**Confirm that what was said has been heard and/or understood** (use of confirmation). [cite: 1290] [cite_start]This provides the space, time, and confidence needed for thinking. [cite: 1291] |
| **Prompting & Timing** | [cite_start]Provide different prompts for thinking, ensuring they are presented at the **right time** in the conversation. [cite: 1292] |
| **Shared Understanding** | [cite_start]Establish **shared understanding of vocabulary**. [cite: 1293] |
| **Power Balance** | Maintain a meaningful power balance. [cite_start]Ensure the non-autistic participant's conversational turns do not become long, as this limits the autistic participant's ability to contribute. [cite: 1294, 1295] |
| **Self-Advocacy (Autistic Partner)** | [cite_start]The autistic participant can take control by **explicitly informing partners about unhelpful approaches** (e.g., stating a dislike for too many questions or specific "feeling words"). [cite: 1296] |

### B. Unhelpful Strategies to Avoid ("Blocks")

[cite_start]These behaviors, often intended to be helpful, impede thinking and contribution. [cite: 1297]

| Strategy Area | Specific Actions/Behaviors to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Questioning** | [cite_start]**Poor timing of questions:** Avoid making a request to think (such as a "why" question, statement, indirect, or multiple question) **before** the autistic person is focused on the topic. [cite: 1298] |
| **Contribution** | [cite_start]**Giving ideas or suggestions**. [cite: 1299] |
| **Interruption** | [cite_start]**Interrupting** the conversation. [cite: 1299] |
| **Ignoring Cues** | [cite_start]Failing to notice and respond to **disengagement**. [cite: 1300] |

## 🏢 Organizational and Workplace Strategies

[cite_start]Organizations can employ specific practices to support diverse interaction styles (like those of Gen Z or employees with ASD). [cite: 1301]

| Organizational Focus Area | Actionable Strategies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Training & Culture** | [cite_start]Provide **tailored sensitivity and awareness training** programs for neurotypical colleagues and managerial staff to foster an inclusive organizational culture. [cite: 1303, 1304] |
| **Communication Modality** | [cite_start]Use **non-direct, electronically mediated communication** (e.g., e-mails, instant messaging, chatbots) to reduce stress for those who find intense verbal contact challenging. [cite: 1305, 1306] |
| **Clarity and Instruction** | [cite_start]Use **concise and precise messages** and clear instructions that eliminate doubt regarding the task, completion method, deadline, and contact person. [cite: 1307] |
| **Documentation** | [cite_start]Follow verbal instructions with the **written form**. [cite: 1308] [cite_start]Utilize **visual forms** (e.g., visual job schedules, instructional pictures). [cite: 1308] |
| **Feedback Delivery** | [cite_start]Deliver feedback that is **direct but sensitive**, avoiding unnecessary emotions that might disturb the communication process. [cite: 1309] |
| **Structure & Meetings** | [cite_start]Introduce structure where possible, provide a task sequence, and **share information about changes in advance**. [cite: 1310] [cite_start]Organize meetings in small groups, providing a **written agenda beforehand and minutes afterward**. [cite: 1311] |
| **Support Systems** | [cite_start]Provide a clear **chain of command** (e.g., contact with one person only). [cite: 1312] [cite_start]Offer support from a **buddy, mentor, or job coach**. [cite: 1313] |
| **Recruitment** | [cite_start]Design **less structured job interviews**. [cite: 1314] [cite_start]Interview questions should be detailed, clearly formulated, and precise, **avoiding abstraction and ambiguity**. [cite: 1315] |

## 🧠 Foundational Principles for Understanding

[cite_start]Effective interactional expertise relies on underlying principles of mindset, culture, and social interaction. [cite: 1316]

### A. Mindset and Psychological Safety

* [cite_start]**Growth Mindset:** Cultivating the belief that qualities (like relationship skills) can be developed creates a passion for learning and perseverance. [cite: 1318]
* [cite_start]**Psychological Safety:** Effective teamwork requires a psychologically safe workplace. [cite: 1319] [cite_start]Leaders must **drive fear out of the organization** to create conditions for learning and innovation, as fear limits effective thought. [cite: 1320]
* [cite_start]**Focus on Collaboration:** View employees as **collaborators** and support their development through coaching and feedback. [cite: 1321, 1322]

### B. Cultural and Contextual Awareness

* [cite_start]**Transcending Culture:** To interact successfully, one must **transcend one's own cultural control system** by making its implicit rules explicit. [cite: 1323, 1324]
* [cite_start]**Contexting:** Recognize that context carries meaning. [cite: 1325]
    * [cite_start]**High-Context (HC) communication** is mostly pre-programmed, with minimal information in the transmitted message; it is resistant to change. [cite: 1326]
    * [cite_start]**Low-Context (LC) communication** has most information in the explicit message; it is fast-evolving and unstable. [cite: 1327]
* [cite_start]**Neurodiversity Paradigm:** Communication difficulties often arise from a **mismatch in neurotypes (the double empathy theory)**, not an inherent deficit. [cite: 1328] [cite_start]Communication between **matched neurotype pairs is significantly more accurate** than in mixed pairs. [cite: 1329, 1330]

### C. Impression Management

* [cite_start]**Performance and Communication:** Activity directed at a work task tends to be **converted into activity oriented toward communication**. [cite: 1331] [cite_start]This representation can be a misrepresentation. [cite: 1332]
* [cite_start]**Defensive and Protective Practices:** Interactants constantly use defensive practices (to protect their *own* projections) and protective practices, or **tact** (to save *another's* projected definition of the situation). [cite: 1333]
* [cite_start]**Synchrony:** People move together in interactions in an unconscious, synchronized way ("being in sync") that is a form of communication. [cite: 1334] [cite_start]Disrupting this synchrony can disrupt communication. [cite: 1335]
