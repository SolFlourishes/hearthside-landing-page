## 💡 Core Concept: "Mental Programs"[cite_start]The book *Culture's Consequences* by Geert Hofstede explores **differences in thinking and social action** among members of more than 50 modern nations[cite: 1230].> [cite_start]It argues that people carry **"mental programs"** that are developed in the family and reinforced in educational and organizational settings[cite: 1231]. [cite_start]These mental programs contain a **component of national culture**[cite: 1231].[cite_start]The research was drawn from a massive IBM attitude survey (over 116,000 questionnaires) to identify empirically verifiable dimensions along which **dominant value systems** vary[cite: 1232, 1233].---## 🧭 The Five Dimensions of Culture[cite_start]The work identifies **five main dimensions** that describe culture and correspond to fundamental problems of human survival that every society must address[cite: 1235, 1247].1.  **Power Distance (PDI):**    > [cite_start]The extent to which less powerful members of institutions **accept and expect unequal power distribution**[cite: 1236]. [cite_start]This addresses the fundamental problem of inequality[cite: 1247].2.  **Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI):**    > [cite_start]The extent to which a culture programs its members to feel **comfortable or uncomfortable in unstructured situations** (novel, unknown, surprising)[cite: 1237]. [cite_start]This addresses the problem of stress in society[cite: 1247].3.  **Individualism versus Collectivism (IDV):**    > [cite_start]The degree to which individuals are expected to **look after themselves or remain integrated into primary groups**[cite: 1238]. [cite_start]This addresses the problem of integrating individuals into groups[cite: 1247].4.  **Masculinity versus Femininity (MAS):**    > [cite_start]Refers to the **distribution of emotional roles** between genders[cite: 1239]. [cite_start]This addresses the fundamental problem of emotional role distribution[cite: 1247].5.  **Long-term versus Short-term Orientation:**    > [cite_start]Relates to the extent to which a culture programs its members to **accept delayed gratification**[cite: 1240]. [cite_start]This addresses the choice of focus for effort[cite: 1247].---## 🗣️ Applications in Communication[cite_start]These cultural dimensions, particularly **Individualism-Collectivism**, are used to explain cross-cultural differences in nonverbal behavior[cite: 1241].### Emotional Expressivity* [cite_start]Studies link cultural differences in "display rules" to culture-level individualism[cite: 1242].* [cite_start]Athletes from urban, **individualistic cultures expressed their emotions more** than athletes from less urban, more collectivistic cultures[cite: 1243].* [cite_start]**Collectivistic cultures** are generally associated with a display rule norm of **less expressivity overall**, which is central to preserving social order[cite: 1244].### Proxemics (Space) and Haptics (Touch)* [cite_start]The individualism-collectivism dimension is used to differentiate cultures[cite: 1245].* [cite_start]Asian cultures, which often emphasize **group harmony**, tend to **discourage public touch**[cite: 1245].* [cite_start]High-latitude cultures, which may value planning over sociability, can result in people who are **less warm and gregarious**[cite: 1246].
