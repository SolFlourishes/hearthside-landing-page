## 💡 The Dramaturgical Framework

[cite_start]The analysis of social life relies on the perspective of **the theatrical performance**, generating principles described as dramaturgical ones[cite: 874]. [cite_start]When an individual (the performer) enters the presence of others (the **audience**), they seek information to **define the situation**[cite: 875].

### The Basic Goal of Performance
[cite_start]The individual's objective is to **control the conduct of the others**, specifically their responsive treatment of him[cite: 876]. [cite_start]This control is achieved mainly by **influencing the definition of the situation** they formulate[cite: 877].

* [cite_start]**Interaction/Encounter:** All the reciprocal influence of individuals on one another's actions during one continuous occasion while in immediate physical presence[cite: 878].
* [cite_start]**Performance:** All the activity of a given participant on a given occasion that serves to influence any of the other participants[cite: 879].
* [cite_start]**Part or Routine:** The pre-established pattern of action unfolded during a performance that may be played through on other occasions[cite: 880].
* [cite_start]**Moral Demand:** When an individual projects a definition of a situation and claims to be a person of a particular kind, he automatically exerts a **moral demand** upon others to treat him accordingly[cite: 881].
* [cite_start]**First Impressions:** The individual's **initial projection commits him** to what he is proposing to be, and these first impressions are crucial for establishing the plan for co-operative activity[cite: 882].

### The Nature of the Performed Self
[cite_start]The origin of the self is tied to performance[cite: 883]. [cite_start]The word "**person**," in its first meaning, is a **mask**, recognizing that everyone is perpetually performing a role[cite: 884].

* [cite_start]**Self as Role:** The mask represents the **conception we have formed of ourselves**—the role we are striving to live up to—and thus, the mask is considered the "truer self"[cite: 885]. [cite_start]Eventually, this conception of one's role becomes **second nature**[cite: 886].
* [cite_start]**Cynicism and Sincerity:** Performers can oscillate between complete belief in their role (**sincerity**) and complete disbelief (**cynicism**)[cite: 887]. [cite_start]Goffman states that the individual automatically becomes **insincere** when adhering to the obligation of maintaining a working consensus[cite: 888].

## 🎭 The Components of the Front

[cite_start]The **Front** is the **expressive equipment of a standard kind** employed by the individual to define the situation for the observers[cite: 889].

| Component | Definition and Function | Supporting Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Setting** | [cite_start]The fixed sign-equipment, including the furniture, decor, and physical background[cite: 891]. | [cite_start]Organizations may "stock" the setting with academically-trained experts to provide an **aura of thought and respectability**[cite: 891, 892]. |
| **Appearance** | [cite_start]Stimuli that function to inform the audience of the performer's **social statuses** (e.g., clothing)[cite: 893]. | [cite_start]Appearance also communicates the individual's temporary ritual state (e.g., work, formal activity, or recreation)[cite: 894]. |
| **Manner** | [cite_start]Stimuli that function to warn the audience of the **interaction role** the performer expects to play (e.g., meek, haughty, or aggressive manner)[cite: 895]. | [cite_start]We commonly expect a **confirming consistency** between appearance and manner[cite: 896]. |
| **Coherence** | [cite_start]A coherence is expected among the setting, appearance, and manner[cite: 897]. [cite_start]The social front allows the observer to place the situation into a **broad category**, easily orienting themselves[cite: 898]. | |

## 🤫 Managing Impressions and Information Control

[cite_start]Since the audience must accept the individual largely on faith, controlling the flow of information is paramount[cite: 899].

### The Idealization Process
[cite_start]The performance tends to offer an impression that is **idealized**[cite: 900]. [cite_start]The performance will incorporate and exemplify the **officially accredited values of the society**, often more so than the performer's general behavior[cite: 901]. [cite_start]This creates a reality that has some characteristics of a **celebration**[cite: 902].

### The Dialectic of Action vs. Expression
[cite_start]A crucial constraint is the conflict between genuinely performing a task and communicating that performance effectively[cite: 903].

* [cite_start]**Conversion of Activity:** Activity directed toward a work-task tends to be **converted into activity oriented toward communication**[cite: 903].
* [cite_start]**Dilemma of Talent:** Those who have the time and talent to perform a task well may not have the time or talent to **make it apparent** that they are performing well[cite: 904].
* [cite_start]**Dramatic Realization:** To be accepted, the performer must **mobilize his activity** to express what he wishes to convey (e.g., a baseball umpire must make an instantaneous decision to ensure the audience is sure of his judgment)[cite: 905, 906].

### Concealment and Misrepresentation
[cite_start]To maintain the idealized front, performers must **forgo or conceal** inconsistent actions[cite: 907].

| Category of Concealment | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Secret Consumption/Pleasures** | [cite_start]Indulging in inappropriate conduct (e.g., watching children's TV) only **secretly** to maintain an impression of maturity[cite: 908]. |
| **Dirty Work** | [cite_start]Concealing all evidence of **physically unclean, semi-illegal, cruel, or degrading tasks** necessary for the final product (e.g., the undertaker concealing the workroom)[cite: 909, 910]. |
| **Hidden Profits/Activities** | [cite_start]Concealing a profitable activity ("outside racket") that is incompatible with the official presentation[cite: 911]. |
| **Polished End-Product** | [cite_start]Showing only the finished, polished product while concealing the **long, tedious hours** of lonely labor[cite: 912]. |
| **Inherent Misrepresentation** | [cite_start]The representation of an activity will **inevitably be a misrepresentation** of the activity itself, as the need to rely on signs for reality creates this possibility[cite: 913, 914]. |

### Teamwork and Regions
[cite_start]Performance is usually a **co-operative effort of a team**[cite: 915].

* [cite_start]**Back Region:** A place **bounded by barriers to perception** where the performance is prepared and where the performer can **relax**, drop their front, and **step out of character**[cite: 915, 916].
* [cite_start]**Front Region:** The location where the performance is presented[cite: 916].
* [cite_start]**Team Solidarity:** In the back region, teammates compare experiences and develop **solidarity**, often maintained by **derogating the absent audience**[cite: 917, 918].
* [cite_start]**Secrecy and Loyalty:** Team members must accept minor parts, not betray secrets, and be sufficiently taken in by their own performance to avoid sounding hollow[cite: 919, 920].

### Defensive and Protective Practices
To stabilize the fragile impression of reality:

* [cite_start]**Defensive Practices:** Strategies and tactics used by the individual to **protect his own projections**[cite: 921].
* [cite_start]**Protective Practices (Tact):** Strategies used by a participant to **save another's projected definition of the situation**[cite: 922]. [cite_start]Audience tact includes avoiding interruptions and inhibiting acts that might create a *faux pas*[cite: 922, 923].

## IV. The Performer's Amoral Role

[cite_start]The individual, as a performer, constantly deals with **moral matters** but does not have a *moral concern* in them[cite: 924].

* [cite_start]**Merchants of Morality:** Performers are **"merchants of morality,"** concerned solely with the **amoral issue of engineering a convincing impression** that these moral standards are being realized[cite: 924, 925].
* [cite_start]**Bureaucratic Fixity:** The performer is expected to assume a **"tragic mask,"** crystallizing his soul into an idea and hiding inequalities of moods[cite: 926]. [cite_start]This demands a **bureaucratization of the spirit** so a homogeneous performance can be relied upon[cite: 927].
* [cite_start]**Flexibility:** The freest person is one who is **flexible** and can choose which strategies to use, rather than slavishly replaying the same script[cite: 928].
