# Mapping the Margins: Intersectionality and Identity Politics

## Defining Intersectionality

**Intersectionality** is a way of framing the various interactions of race and gender in the context of violence against women of color. More broadly, intersectionality is useful as a method for **mediating the tension between assertions of multiple identity and the ongoing necessity of group politics**.

## Identity Politics and Antiessentialism

Intersectionality is distinguished from **antiessentialism**—the perspective from which women of color have critically engaged white feminism for the absence of women of color and speaking for women of color.

One interpretation is that feminism **essentializes the category "woman"**. This critique owes a great deal to the **postmodernist idea** that categories typically regarded as natural are, in fact, **socially constructed in a linguistic economy of difference**.

## Contextual Critique

Mainstream society handles modes associated with identity that resist absorption differently. Cultural modes that remain distinctive are either neglected or dismissed as "deviant".

### The Element of Resistance

Rap music, even if misogynistic, may hold **political value as a discourse of resistance**. The element of resistance is found in its ability to **make people uncomfortable**, thereby challenging received habits of thought and action.

Against the historical backdrop of the Black male as a social outlaw, **"gangsta' rap"** might be taken as a **rejection of a conciliatory stance**, favoring a more **subversive form of opposition**.

## Implications for Political Discourse

- Presenting information outside of its **fuller context** hampers complex understanding
- **Essentializing one dimension** to stand for the whole is problematic
- Different modes of resistance may be misunderstood when evaluated through a single lens
- Context, nuance, and ambiguity are essential for understanding across divides
