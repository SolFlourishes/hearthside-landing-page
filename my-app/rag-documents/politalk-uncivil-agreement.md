# Uncivil Agreement: How Politics Became Our Identity

## The Crisis of Identity-Based Democracy

American politics has transformed from policy debates into tribal warfare. The American electorate has become **deeply socially divided along partisan lines**, creating an environment where norms of racial, religious, and cultural respect deteriorate.

### Social Polarization

**Social polarization** is a phenomenon where American partisans are prone to **stereotyping, prejudice, and emotional volatility**. Partisan conflict today is increasingly characterized by an **exaggerated and poorly understood difference** between the parties, rooted in the **same impulses that drive racial and religious prejudice**.

When people limit exposure to outgroup individuals, the perceived differences between parties grow increasingly exaggerated. When everything is perceived to be at stake, there is no room for quibbles about character, truth, or principles. The electorate is increasingly moved to action by a **desire for victory that exceeds their desire for the greater good**.

### Social Sorting

**Social sorting** is the process by which partisan, ideological, religious, and racial identities have moved into **strong alignment**. This results in each party growing **increasingly socially homogeneous**. The alignment of these identities has **degraded the cross-cutting social ties** that once allowed for partisan compromise.

When parties become more socially homogeneous, their members are **quicker to anger and tend toward intolerance**. This homogenization contributes to the feeling that "everything is in play," and compromise or reasoned disagreement grows increasingly difficult.

## The Loss of Cross-Pressures

Historically, **cross-cutting cleavages** (or cross-pressures) were necessary to mitigate social conflict. Cross-pressures occur when individuals or groups are subject to conflicting social pressures that operate in opposing directions.

Voters subject to cross-cutting social identities tend to be a source of **flexibility in policy responses** and serve as a **buffer against social polarization**. Those with cross-cutting identities tend to be more prepared to **engage socially** with partisan opponents.

In contrast, those with **highly aligned** religious, racial, and partisan identities are **less prepared to engage** with their partisan opponents.

## Psychological Effects

**Political identity** is increasingly driving political **thoughts, emotions, and actions** ahead of policy preferences. The phenomenon of **motivated reasoning** suggests that individuals rationalize their choices in a way that is consistent with **what they prefer to believe**, rather than evaluating objective reality.

In this tribal world, people are increasingly blind to objective reality and prone to motivated reasoning.
