## 💡 Core Concepts and the Shift in PunishmentMichel Foucault's *Discipline and Punish* provides a **correlative history of the modern soul and of a new power to judge**. It analyzes the metamorphosis of punitive methods based on a **political technology of the body**.The book is organized into four parts: **Torture, Punishment, Discipline, and Prison**.### The Key Historical ShiftBetween the late 18th and early 19th centuries, the **entire economy of punishment was redistributed**, marked primarily by the **disappearance of torture as a public spectacle**.* **Shift in Target:** The punitive operation shifted its objective from addressing the **body** to striking the **"soul"** (heart, thoughts, will, inclinations).* **Shift in Method:** Physical pain ceased to be the constituent element of the penalty, which became an **"economy of suspended rights"**.* **Purpose of Reform:** The reform aimed to set up a new **"economy" of the power to punish**—to make it more regular, effective, and continuous, while reducing its economic and political costs.---## 🔥 The Old System: Punishment as Spectacle (Supplice)*Supplice* (torture) was the punitive method of the old system.* **Nature of Torture:** It was a **differentiated production of pain** and an **organized ritual** intended to mark the victim with infamy. It rested on a quantitative art of pain, correlating the intensity and duration of pain with the gravity of the crime.* **Political Function:** The public execution was a **juridico-political ritual** by which injured **sovereignty was reconstituted**. It functioned as a **policy of terror**, manifesting the invincible force of the sovereign.* **Decline of Spectacle:** Public executions were eventually seen as dangerous because they risked **accustoming spectators to a ferocity** or making the executioner resemble a criminal.* **Modern Judicial Attitude:** As public executions disappeared, punishment became the **most hidden part of the penal process**, leading to a **shame in punishing** on the part of modern justice.---## 🧠 The New System: Discipline and the SoulIn modern penality, judgement is passed not only on the "crime" but also on the criminal's **passions, instincts, anomalies, maladjustments, drives, and desires**. These are the **"shadows lurking behind the case itself that are judged and punished"**.### The New Truth in TrialThe question is no longer simply "Has the act been established and is it punishable?" but also **"What is this act?** Is it a phantasy, a psychotic reaction, a delusional episode, a perverse action?".### The Role of ExpertsA whole set of **assessing, diagnostic, prognostic, normative judgements** concerning the criminal have become lodged in the framework of penal judgement. The psychiatric expert acts as an **adviser on punishment**, determining if the subject is "dangerous" and suggesting a prescription for his "medico-judicial treatment".### The Soul as an InstrumentThe "soul," in this context, is not a theological substance but an element born out of **methods of punishment, supervision, and constraint**. It is the **effect and instrument of a political anatomy**.> Foucault states: **"the soul is the prison of the body"**.---## ⛓️ Discipline and the Carceral System### Discipline Defined> **Disciplines** are methods that assure the **constant subjection of bodily forces** and impose upon them a **relation of docility-utility**.Discipline is a **political anatomy of detail**, which produces **subjected and practiced bodies, "docile" bodies**.### Disciplinary MechanismsDiscipline relies on simple instruments: **hierarchical observation, normalizing judgement, and the examination**.* **Normalizing Judgement:** Disciplinary punishment is aimed at **non-observance**—that which departs from the rule. This penality **compares, differentiates, hierarchizes, homogenizes, and excludes**; in short, it **normalizes**.* **The Examination:** The examination is a **normalizing gaze** that combines hierarchical observation and normalizing judgement. It makes each individual a **"case,"** which constitutes an object for knowledge and a hold for power.### Panopticism**Bentham’s Panopticon** is the architectural figure of discipline.* **Main Effect:** To induce in the inmate a state of **conscious and permanent visibility** that ensures the **automatic functioning of power**.* **Application:** It is polyvalent, applicable to prisons, hospitals, schools, and factories.### The Prison as ApparatusThe prison is an **"omni-disciplinary"** apparatus, a complete and austere institution intended to be an **uninterrupted** mechanism exercising a **despotic discipline**.* **Foundation of Imprisonment:** The prison's self-evident nature is based on its simple form of **"deprivation of liberty"** (an egalitarian punishment) and its ability to quantify penalty by **time**.* **Isolation Principle:** Isolation of the convict (from the world and each other) is the **primary condition of total submission** and a **positive instrument of reform** that allows the convict to reflect on his crime.* **Work Principle:** Penal labor is defined as an agent of carceral transformation, serving as a **principle of order and regularity**. It is the **machinery that transforms the violent delinquent** into a docile worker.### Delinquency and Failure* **Creation of the "Delinquent":** The penitentiary system does not simply punish offenders; it fabricates the **"delinquent,"** a character distinct from the offender by his life, instincts, and drives. The prison fabricates delinquents by introducing this non-corporal reality into the law.* **Failure as Function:** The failure of the prison (i.e., the increase of crime and recidivism) may be intentional. The prison is not intended to eliminate offenses, but to **"distinguish them, to distribute them, to use them,"** thereby controlling illegalities and segmenting a **manipulable delinquency**.
