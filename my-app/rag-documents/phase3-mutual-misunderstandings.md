## 💡 Core Concept: Mutual (Mis)understandingThis concept reframes autistic pragmatic "impairments" by drawing on the **Double Empathy Problem (DEP)**.> The **DEP** posits that cross-dispositional communication (between different neurotypes) is troubled by a **“disjuncture in reciprocity”** between social actors who hold different norms and expectations.Misunderstanding is viewed as a **mutual failure** in reaching consensus through bidirectional empathy, rather than solely a consequence of autistic impairment.---## 🧠 Theoretical Mechanism: Relevance Theory (RT)Mutual (mis)understanding is explained using **Relevance Theory (RT)**, a cognitive account of utterance interpretation.> RT defines communication success as the retrieval of intended meanings through a search for **optimal relevance**—the greatest number of cognitive effects achieved for the lowest amount of processing effort.### The Crucial Cognitive Element: Mutual ManifestnessThe basis for judging optimal relevance is **"mutual manifestness"** (or the lack thereof).* **Definition:** Mutual manifestness refers to the **shared assumptions** within a **mutual cognitive environment**.* **Breakdown:** Mutual misunderstanding breaks down where **assumptions of mutual manifestness are erroneously made** by either or both parties.### Root Cause of MismatchAutistic and non-autistic people may attribute relevance differently due to:* **Divergent sensory and perceptual experiences**.* Markedly different **patterns of attention**.What is salient to each person may be mismatched, requiring **extra cognitive effort** to achieve mutual understanding. This aligns with the **Monotropism** theory, where differences in narrowly focused attention affect processing and salience.---## 📊 Empirical Findings and Strategies### Findings from Williams et al. StudyIn a study analyzing conversations between autistic and non-autistic adults, **mutual understanding was unexpectedly abundant** across all pairings. However, **qualitative differences** were discernible.* **Successful Understanding (Matched Neurotype):**    * **Matched-dispositional conversations (Autistic + Autistic)** were characterized by a significant increase in **flow, rapport, and intersubjective attunement**.    * This suggests autistic people experience minimized barriers to social communication when engaging with others of a similar neurotype, supporting the DEP.* **Successful Strategies (Matched Neurotype):**    * High mutual manifestness made understanding **less effortful**, increasing available cognitive resources.    * When two monotropic individuals synchronize their attention, a **"hyper-confluence of cognitive environments"** may occur, accompanied by increased affective reward.### Mitigating Misunderstanding* **Barrier-free communication** provides equal opportunities for mutual understanding by requiring interlocutors to invest **well-balanced effort** proportional to their own limits.* Strategies used to achieve mutual understanding include **avoiding figurative speech**, requiring **direct feedback**, and using **plain, easy language**.* **Learning neurotypical strategies** (masking/camouflaging) is also a strategy used by autistic individuals to fit social norms.
