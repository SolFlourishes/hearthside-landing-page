# Civics at Work: Promoting Civil Discourse in Professional Settings

## The Three C's

The essential issues for promoting democracy are the "Three C's": **civics, civility, and collaboration**. These are the **cornerstones of democracy**. Civility enables difficult conversations to be productive, which leads to a more just, equitable, and secure society.

## Why the Workplace Matters

The workplace is an important setting for promoting civil discourse because it is one of the few places where people do not typically **self-sort**. Employees often sit next to colleagues with different political views but share a common mission.

Workplace civil discourse yields **better products for customers** because it relies on sharing diverse perspectives.

## Strategies for Promoting Civil Discourse

### Focus on Shared Aspirations

Start by identifying shared aspirations (e.g., "government treating everyone fairly") or the shared company mission. Acknowledging agreement makes it harder to "hate the other person".

### Civil Dialogue vs. Debate

Dialogue should focus on **listening and understanding**, rather than winning or convincing the opponent (which is debate).

### Empower Agency

Civics is vital because it empowers individuals to be **effective agents of change**. When people feel they cannot bring about change, they may settle for "just yelling" (emoting) to express frustration.

### Complicate the Narrative

When discussing polarizing issues, **complicate the narrative** by featuring nuance, contradiction, and ambiguity. Presenting issues in simple, pro-versus-con terms can backfire.

### Address Topics Proactively

Conversations are inevitable, so leaders must take proactive steps to make them civil rather than ignoring them.

## Building Emotional Reservoirs

Establishing rapport or positivity early in tense encounters is crucial. Positive emotions widen attention and broaden behavioral repertoires.

## Learning from Successes

Instead of focusing on problems, look for and build on "positive pockets of self-healing potential" or "networks of effective action" already working in polarized communities.
